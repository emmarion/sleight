Your task is to prove in Sleight that the fifth busy beaver number is
47,176,870, against the goal in bb5/spec.sl. Or: to lay the groundwork for a
future session to do so. This task will likely take many sessions, so leaving
solid, well-recorded progress matters more than finishing in this one.

The machines are Turing machines with two symbols. A machine with n states,
numbered from 0, has one entry for each state and symbol: either `Halt`, or a
symbol to write, a direction to move and a state to go to. It starts in state 0
on a tape of all 0s, and each step reads the symbol under the head and follows
its entry; a step that reaches a `Halt` entry is the last, and counts.
bb5/defs.sl defines this as `HaltsIn n m c k`, and `BusyBeaver n v`: a machine
with n states that halts in v steps, and a proof that no machine with n states
halts in more. bb5/spec.sl sets the goal `(BusyBeaver 5 47176870)`.

bb5/bb2.sl, bb5/bb3.sl and bb5/bb4.sl set the goals for 2, 3 and 4 states,
whose values are 6, 21 and 107. They are steps along the way, not the task.
Compiling one of them with `sl`, after std/nat.sl, std/list.sl, std/macro.sl,
std/tactics.sl and bb5/defs.sl and before your files, checks a `qed` against
its goal.

To check your work, call `check` with std/nat.sl, std/list.sl, std/macro.sl,
std/tactics.sl, bb5/defs.sl, bb5/spec.sl, then your files, one of them proving
the goal with `(qed e)`. The finished proof must compile with no warnings, and
none of your files may contain a `;! suppress`; intermediate work may have both.

For reference: MANUAL.md (the implementations and their command lines),
WARNINGS.md (the warnings the compilers emit), DESIGN.md (the language),
prelude.sl (the standard library), std/ (source libraries, among them
std/nat.sl, which holds `Le`, and std/list.sl, lemmas about `Length`, `Nth`,
`SetNth` and `Repeat`), tests/ (example programs), and self/ (the compiler,
itself written in Sleight).

Only work/, HANDOFF.md, and FEEDBACK.md carry over to the next session.
Generally put experiments and `sl` captures in scratch/, which does not carry
over; helper programs worth keeping can go in work/. Keep work/ tidy, pruning
files that have served their purpose.

Your session has two phases: first experimenting, then writing HANDOFF.md and
FEEDBACK.md. HANDOFF.md tells the next session where things stand and what to
try next; rewrite it each session instead of adding to it. FEEDBACK.md tells
Sleight's maintainers what slowed you down because of Sleight, its library, or its tools,
rather than because of the problem itself; add to it each session instead of
rewriting it. Each phase has its own budget of tokens. In the second phase,
those two are the only files you can edit; there's no need to edit them, or to
read FEEDBACK.md, before then, so spend the first phase on the task itself.

Hint: `native-compute` (DESIGN.md §72) runs a call in run position as compiled
code, and its result's erased fields come back as proofs.
