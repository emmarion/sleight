Your task is to write a fast, in-place heapsort in SL, certified correct against
heapsort-fast/spec.sl. Or: to lay the groundwork for a future session to do so.
This task will likely take many sessions, so leaving solid, well-recorded
progress matters more than finishing in this one.

To check your work, compile in order: heapsort-fast/spec.sl, your files, then
heapsort-fast/accept.sl. This must compile with no warnings. A solution is not
finished while any array write could be an `-imm` one, any runtime arithmetic
could be a `u62-*` call, or any runtime recursion could be a tail call, even if
measurements show no gain.

For reference: DESIGN.md, UNIQUE.md, SUGAR.md, and LITERALS.md (the language),
prelude.sl (the standard library), tests/ (example programs), and self/ (the
compiler, itself written in SL).

Only work/, HANDOFF.md, and FEEDBACK.md carry over to the next session. In
work/, keep files.txt, listing in order the files that go between
heapsort-fast/spec.sl and the accept file, and main.sl, a driver that sorts a
pseudo-random array and prints a checksum. Generally put experiments and `sl`
captures in scratch/, which does not carry over; helper programs worth keeping
can go in work/. Keep work/ tidy, pruning files that have served their purpose.

Your session has two phases: first experimenting, then writing HANDOFF.md and
FEEDBACK.md. HANDOFF.md tells the next session where things stand and what to
try next. FEEDBACK.md tells SL's maintainers what slowed you down because of SL,
its library, or its tools, rather than because of the problem itself. Each phase
has its own budget of tokens. In the second phase, those two are the only files
you can edit; there's no need to edit them before then, so spend the first phase
on the task itself.
