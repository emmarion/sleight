Your task is to write a fast, certified implementation of Manacher's algorithm in SL.
Or: to lay the groundwork for a future session to do so. This task will likely take many sessions, so leaving solid,
well-recorded progress matters more than finishing in this one.

pal/ holds a certified but slow one; see pal/README.md. Write `longest-palindrome-fast`, certified against the unchanged
`LongestPalindrome` of pal/palindrome_relations.sl, with the interface that manacher-fast/accept-list.sl or
manacher-fast/accept-array.sl expects. You may reuse anything in pal/. Fast here means making good use of arrays, U62
arithmetic, tail calls and minimizing allocations; there is no time target. The `sl` tool's `gc_stats` reports how much a run allocates.

To check your work, compile in order: self/base.sl, pal/palindrome_relations.sl, manacher-fast/below.sl, your files (and
any pal/ files they use), then one accept file. This must compile with no warning that a declaration shadows another.

For reference: prelude.sl (the standard library), self/ (the compiler, written in SL), tests/ (example programs),
DESIGN.md, SUGAR.md and UNIQUE.md.

Only work/ and HANDOFF.md carry over to the next session. In work/, keep files.txt, listing in order the files that go
between manacher-fast/below.sl and the accept file, and main.sl, a driver like pal/manacher_main.sl. Small helper
programs in work/ are fine.

Your session has two phases: first experimenting, then writing HANDOFF.md, which tells the next session where things
stand and what to try next. Each phase has its own budget of tokens, and every tool result says how much is left. In
the second phase, HANDOFF.md is the only file you can edit; there's no need to edit it before then, so spend the first phase on the task
itself.
