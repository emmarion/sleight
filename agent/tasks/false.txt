Your task is to prove the goal in false/spec.sl. Or: to lay the groundwork for a
future session to do so. This task will likely take many
sessions, so leaving solid, well-recorded progress matters more than finishing
in this one.

To check your work, call `check` with false/spec.sl, then your files, one of
them proving its goal, `(False)`, with `(qed e)`. Warnings are allowed.

A function into `(False)` is ordinary ex falso: it shows its arguments can't
exist, and a consistent logic allows it. What the goal needs is a value
of a type that should be empty. Look for that value, not for functions into
`(False)`.

For reference: DESIGN.md (the language); MANUAL.md (the implementations and
their command lines); WARNINGS.md (the warnings the compilers emit);
ASSUMPTIONS.md (choices the implementations made where DESIGN.md is silent);
BUGS.md, OPEN.md, and SPEC-REVIEW.md (known defects and open questions);
lib/prelude.sl (the standard library); std/ (source libraries of lemmas);
tests/ (example programs); pal/ (a certified program); self/ (the compiler
that the `sl` tool runs, itself written in Sleight); and lib/ and bin/ (the
reference compiler, in OCaml).

Only work/, HANDOFF.md, and FEEDBACK.md carry over to the next session.
Generally put experiments and `sl` captures in scratch/, which does not carry
over; helper programs worth keeping can go in work/. Keep work/ tidy, pruning
files that have served their purpose.

Your session has two phases: first experimenting, then writing HANDOFF.md and
FEEDBACK.md. HANDOFF.md tells the next session where things stand and what to
try next; rewrite it each session instead of adding to it. FEEDBACK.md tells
Sleight's maintainers each suspected inconsistency you found, with the smallest program that
shows it, and anything about Sleight, its library, or its tools that slowed you
down; add to it each session instead of rewriting it. Each phase has its own
budget of tokens. In the second phase, those two are the only files you can
edit; there's no need to edit them, or to read FEEDBACK.md, before then, so
spend the first phase on the task itself.
