Your task is to find a soundness hole in SL by proving False. Or: to lay the
groundwork for a future session to do so. This task will likely take many
sessions, so leaving solid, well-recorded progress matters more than finishing
in this one.

To check your work, compile in order: your files, then false/accept.sl. It
calls `bottom`, a function you define that takes no arguments and returns
`(erased (False) f)`. Warnings are allowed.

For reference: DESIGN.md (the language); BUGS.md, OPEN.md, and SPEC-REVIEW.md
(known defects and open questions); lib/prelude.sl (the standard library);
tests/ (example programs); pal/ (a certified program); self/ (the compiler
that the `sl` tool runs, itself written in SL); and lib/ and bin/ (the
reference compiler, in OCaml).

Only work/, HANDOFF.md, and FEEDBACK.md carry over to the next session. In
work/, keep files.txt, listing in order the files that go before
false/accept.sl. Generally put experiments and `sl` captures in scratch/,
which does not carry over. Keep work/ tidy, pruning files that have served
their purpose.

Your session has two phases: first experimenting, then writing HANDOFF.md and
FEEDBACK.md. HANDOFF.md tells the next session where things stand and what to
try next; rewrite it each session instead of adding to it. FEEDBACK.md tells
SL's maintainers each suspected hole you found, with the smallest program that
shows it, and anything about SL, its library, or its tools that slowed you
down; add to it each session instead of rewriting it. Each phase has its own
budget of tokens. In the second phase, those two are the only files you can
edit; there's no need to edit them, or to read FEEDBACK.md, before then, so
spend the first phase on the task itself.
